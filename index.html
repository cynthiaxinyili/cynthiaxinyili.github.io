About

I am an undergraduate at Carnegie Mellon University studying economics and mathematical sciences. I am applying to PhD programs in statistics and biostatistics.

I am interested in statistical methods for social networks and biomedical data. I work on models for social networks, including local network autocorrelation and multiplex network models, and on spatial inference for single-cell spatial transcriptomics in Alzheimer's disease. I have been fortunate to work with Prof. Nynke Niezink and Prof. Weijing Tang at CMU and Prof. Jiebiao Wang at the University of Pittsburgh.

I have presented my work at CMU Networkshop, at CMU's Meeting of the Minds symposium, and at a biostatistics research group at the University of Pittsburgh, and I will present a poster at the 2026 Conference on Statistical Learning and Data Science in New York. I also regularly attend Networkshop, a weekly statistical network analysis group at CMU.

Research

Local network autocorrelation

With Prof. Nynke Niezink, CMU Statistics · 2025–present

Adapts local autocorrelation measures from spatial statistics, local Moran's I and local join counts, to social networks to capture individual-level variation in attribute similarity. Evaluated with stochastic actor-oriented model simulations and permutation tests, and applied to longitudinal friendship data on stress, alcohol use, and smoking.

Li, C. X. and Niezink, N. M. D. Local Network Autocorrelation: Uncovering Individual Heterogeneity in Attribute Clustering. Submitted to Sociological Methods & Research.

Multiplex p2 models for mixed networks

With Prof. Nynke Niezink, CMU Statistics · 2026–present · R package multip2

Extends the Bayesian multiplex p2 model to networks with both directed and undirected layers, deriving the dyad-level likelihood for mixed multiplex networks and implementing it in the multip2 package.

Spatial inference in Alzheimer's disease

With Prof. Jiebiao Wang, Pitt Biostatistics · 2026–present

Spatial analysis of single-cell spatial transcriptomics data to study how cell–cell and cell–pathology relationships change with disease progression, using KAMP-based colocalization inference, functional data analysis across spatial scales, and network-based measures of cell-type adjacency.

Item response theory for evaluating LLMs

With Prof. Weijing Tang, CMU Statistics · 2025

Extended IRT-based LLM evaluation to multidimensional latent abilities with embedding-based item parameters, comparing maximum likelihood and Monte Carlo EM estimation in simulation.
